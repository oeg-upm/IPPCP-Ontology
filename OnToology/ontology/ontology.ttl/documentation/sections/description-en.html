The Emissions Inventory Ontology: Description back to Table of Contents

The Emissions Inventory Ontology (EIO) models emission assessments and the contextual information required to interpret them. The following diagram provides an overview of the ontology’s main concepts and relationships.

Emission assessments

The central concept is s4city:KeyPerformanceIndicatorAssessment , which represents an individual emission assessment or reported emission data item.

Each assessment may specify:

  • the emission indicator through s4city:quantifiesKPI ;
  • the geographical feature through s4city:refersToFeature ;
  • the reporting period through s4city:refersToTime ;
  • the emission sector through eio:refersToSector ;
  • the numerical value through saref:hasValue ;
  • the unit of measurement through saref:isMeasuredIn .

Emission sectors

Emission sectors are represented by eio:Sector and organised hierarchically using skos:broader . This structure makes it possible to connect each sector with its direct and indirect subsectors.

Spatial and temporal context

The ontology supports geographical entities such as s4city:City , s4city:District , and eio:Cell.

Temporal information is represented using time:DateTimeInterval , time:DateTimeDescription , and time:year .

Reused ontologies and vocabularies

EIO reuses terms from established ontologies and vocabularies to promote interoperability and avoid redefining general-purpose concepts:

  • SAREF and SAREF4CITY for values, units, geographical features, KPI assessments, and temporal references;
  • OWL-Time for reporting periods and calendar years;
  • SKOS for hierarchical sector classifications;
  • QUDT for units of measurement.

In summary, EIO defines the domain-specific emission indicators and sector classifications required by an emissions inventory, while relying on established external vocabularies for more general concepts such as measurements, units, locations, time intervals, and hierarchical relationships.
